---
title: "1994: Harlan Tour to England & Ireland"
---

At the Harlan Family in America Tricentennial gathering in
Delaware in 1987, interest was shown for an organized
Harlan tour to England and Ireland. As a result, Marjory
Harlan Sgori of New York planned and led a group of 80 Harlans
on a tour in 1994.

The group flew from Newark, New Jersey, to London. The
highlight of the first evening was riding "the tube" to Harrod's.
On a bus tour of London the next day, we saw the Parliament
building, Big Ben, Tower of London, and Buckingham Palace.

On the second full day in London, we visited Westminster Abbey.
We also toured Bunhill Fields Burial Ground, which is the resting
site of many famous 16th-19th century English intellectuals,
radicals, and clergymen. Leaving London by bus, we stopped at
Althorp, the family home of Diana, Princess Of Wales. We then
visited the York Minster, viewing its beautiful stained-glass
windows. The JORVIK Viking Center in York gave us a glimpse
into life in the year 960 AD.

The following day, we headed to Sutton Park, Sutton-on-the-Forest, home of Richard Harland.
Across the street is All Hollows Church where the Harland’s
worshiped and are buried. Silver serving pieces on the altar,
still used today, were given to the church by the Harland
family. There is a marble tablet mentioning Richard Harland.
After this trip, the Harlan Family in America sent a gift to the
church restoration project. We toured Harwood Castle before
checking into our hotel in Durham.

The next day, we traveled on and attended a church service at St. Peter’s church at Monkwearmouth in Sunderland. George Harlan (#3), was
baptized in this church. We then traveled to the Lake District
where we took a cruise on Lake Windermere before checking
into our hotel. In the evening, we attended a Welsh banquet at
Ruthin Castle.

Then it was on to Belfast, Ireland. We were
greeted at City Hall by the present Lord Mayor. He showed us a
portrait of Sir Edward Harland who was Lord Mayor in 1885/86.
We went to the Harland and Wolfe Shipyards where the Titanic
was built. After that Killyleagh Castle, Castle Ward, and
Carrickfergus Castle were toured. The highlight of the day was
visiting the Quaker meeting house in Lurgan where George
Harlan (#3) and Elizabeth Duck’s marriage was recorded in 1678. Lunch was served to us providing time to visit with 5
Harlands who are still members of the meeting. The following
day, we traveled up the beautiful Antrum Coast Road to the
Giant's Causeway. We were all amazed by the basalt column
formations at the Causeway. At the end of the day, we toured
Broughton Castle, built in 1300, and met Lord and Lady Saye
and Sele.

We left Northern Ireland on the final day and arrived
in Liverpool. We continued to London via the Cotswolds.
Arriving in London, we said our goodbyes and boarded our
flights home. What a memorable trip through England and
Ireland with so much Harlan family history.
